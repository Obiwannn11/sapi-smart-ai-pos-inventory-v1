# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary: Small restaurant and warung owners in Indonesia — non-technical UMKM operators who run their own outlet day-to-day. They are not software people. They open the app in the morning, work through a shift, and close at night. Their relationship with the tool is transactional and repetitive.

Secondary: Cashier staff operating the POS on mobile. They work under time pressure, often in a busy counter or kitchen environment. Errors are costly in front of customers.

Context: The owner views the web dashboard on a desktop or tablet after hours or during quiet moments. The cashier operates the POS on a phone or tablet during active service.

Note: A brand/marketing landing surface is also planned (separate from the product app). That surface will follow brand-register rules. The default here is the product app.

## Product Purpose

SAPI is a multi-tenant POS and inventory management system. It handles the full daily operational loop: opening a cash drawer session, taking and completing transactions, managing product and stock data, and closing out with financial reports. Success means a warung owner can start their day, run their business, and close with full reconciliation — without ever consulting a manual or calling support.

## Brand Personality

Smart, Efficient, Honest.

Voice: Direct and Indonesian. Tool-forward — talks to business owners as capable adults who want to get things done. Doesn't over-promise, doesn't charm, doesn't gamify. When it speaks, it says something useful. The product earns trust by being right, not by being friendly.

Emotional goal: When an owner encounters SAPI, they should feel capable and in control — not impressed, not sold to, just equipped.

## Anti-references

- **Generic Tailwind SaaS clone**: Indigo hero, three icon-text feature cards, "Start Free Trial" CTA, could be any B2B tool ever made. Zero identity.
- **Booble / Majoo / Olsera style**: Bright, promotional, feature-list heavy, discount badges everywhere. Feels like a promo leaflet, not a product. Rushing to impress rather than demonstrate value.
- **Enterprise / ERP landing page**: Dense, gray-on-gray, built for procurement committees, not a cafe owner on their phone.
- **Consumer fintech app**: Big gradient hero, neon accents, gamified rewards, feels like a wallet app (GoPay, OVO) rather than a business operations tool.
- **Western startup cream** (Notion / Linear aesthetic): clean-minimal import that reads as foreign to Indonesian business culture.

## Design Principles

1. **Zero steps to task.** The daily workflow — open cash, take order, close session — must never require thought. Every screen should have an obvious primary action. Nothing buried.

2. **Familiar over novel.** Non-technical owners are not exploring the product. Every interaction should feel like something they already know how to do. Surprise is friction.

3. **Speed is the product.** Hesitation anywhere — a slow load, an animation that lingers, a confirmation that delays — breaks trust. Fast means instant feedback, not just visual smoothness.

4. **Indonesian by default.** Currency format (Rp), payment types (QRIS, bank transfer, tunai), date/time locale, and copy should feel native, not translated from English defaults.

5. **Density with air.** POS tools must show data. But cramped = stressed. Every dense view earns its density; every list gets room to breathe between items.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Standard contrast requirements apply. The cashier interface should support generous touch targets (min 44px) for use on mobile under active-service conditions.
